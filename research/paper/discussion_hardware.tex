The amplifier is not the limit. The noise, resolution and sampling rate an ADS1299 class part gives sit far inside what the decoder tolerates; so do the clock alignment the protocol achieves between two earbuds (about 10~ms, against \hwSkewHolds~ms that cost nothing) and the frame loss a Bluetooth link allows (the specification asks for at most 1\%; scattered loss of \hwLossHolds{} of frames cost under a point). What the decoder does not tolerate are speech envelopes misaligned with the EEG by a few tens of milliseconds and movement jolts left in the signal. Both are requirements on timing and software rather than on parts: the microphones' envelopes have to be stamped on the same clock as the electrodes, to about 10~ms, and the phone's chain gates movement out, as it now does.
